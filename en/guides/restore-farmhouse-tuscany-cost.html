How much does it cost to restore a farmhouse or villa in Tuscany?

What it costs in 2026 to restore a casale, a villa or a country estate in Tuscany: price per square metre by finish, the main line items, fees, contingency and a 200 m² example.

Published 28 November 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

In 2026, restoring a farmhouse or villa in Tuscany costs roughly €600–900 per m² for a light refresh, €1,200–1,800 per m² for a complete standard renovation and €1,800–3,000+ for a higher-specification turnkey job (EmmePi estimates). A 200 m² casale therefore needs about €240,000–360,000 for a complete renovation, plus 15–20% contingency and professional fees.

A Tuscan farmhouse (casale or colonica) is rarely a simple renovation: thick stone walls, old timber roofs, outbuildings and long access roads all show up in the bill. This guide, written in November 2026 by the staged.ai editorial team, brings together the published figures we could verify: Tuscan per-m² estimates from the architecture practice EmmePi Architettura (Pietrasanta), reported by Your Overseas Home in September 2026, and unit costs from the 2026 price guide of Studio Madera, an engineering practice in Florence. All amounts are in euros; one square metre is about 10.8 sq ft.

Does a villa cost more to restore than a farmhouse?

Per square metre, the difference is mostly the finish, not the building type. A farmhouse restored to a good standard sits in the complete standard band; many villa projects aim for premium finishes and land in the higher-spec turnkey band. What changes the total most is the state of the roof and structure, how many outbuildings you convert, and access: a site at the end of a white road (strada bianca) costs more to supply and to clear.

Studio Madera notes that heavy works touching the roof, floors and crawl space add at least 30% to a medium renovation. EmmePi's permit fees are its own professional fees, not national tariffs. Which permit you need depends on the works: a CILA for non-structural work, a SCIA for structural work and a building permit (permesso di costruire) for major changes; in rural and protected landscapes the town hall may require extra approvals.

How much contingency should you keep for an old farmhouse?

More than for a city flat. EmmePi advises 10–15% for straightforward work, 15–20% for older rural property and about 20% for a full transformation. Stone walls, timber beams and floors laid directly on the ground often hide damp, rot or cracks that only appear once work starts. EmmePi's Laura Orlovic adds that on some farmhouses with land the budget ends up about 25% for the purchase and 75% for the renovation, though she stresses this isn't a Tuscan rule of thumb.

Are there tax breaks or reduced VAT?

Qualifying residential renovation work is generally invoiced at 10% VAT instead of 22%. Italy's renovation tax deduction is 50% of eligible spending on your main home and 36% otherwise, up to €96,000 per property unit, as income-tax relief rather than a grant (Italian Revenue Agency, as reported by Your Overseas Home). Because the deduction only reduces Italian income tax, an owner with little Italian taxable income may gain little from it: check with an Italian accountant before you count on it.

How do you plan a farmhouse restoration from abroad?

  1. Get a geometra or architect to check the property before you buy: land registry, planning compliance and the state of the roof and structure.
  2. Decide the level for each building: main house, barn, annexe. Not everything needs the same finish.
  3. Apply the per-m² band above for a first total, then add contingency and fees.
  4. Visualise the result from photos so you and the builders are pricing the same idea.
  5. Ask for at least three quotes on the same specification (capitolato) and agree payment stages in writing.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to restore a farmhouse in the Florence hills?

There is no separate price list for the hills around Florence, but 2026 Tuscan estimates from the architecture practice EmmePi, reported by Your Overseas Home, put a light refresh at €600–900 per m², a complete standard renovation at €1,200–1,800 per m² and a higher-specification turnkey job at €1,800–3,000+. For a 200 m² farmhouse that is roughly €240,000–360,000 for a complete renovation, before a 15–20% contingency.

How much does it cost to restore a villa in the Siena countryside?

Use the same Tuscan bands and pick the one that matches the finish you want. Many villa projects aim for the higher-specification turnkey level, which EmmePi puts at €1,800–3,000+ per m², so a 200 m² villa would start around €360,000 and complex projects can go well beyond that. Access, the state of the roof and structure, and the outbuildings change the total most.

How much does it cost to restore a country estate in Tuscany?

Price each building separately. Apply the per-m² band to the main house, add any barns or annexes you will convert, then the outdoor works. EmmePi advises a contingency of 15–20% for older rural property, plus professional fees: works supervision alone is typically 3–8% of the construction cost. Treat the result as a range to test the purchase, not a quote.

How much contingency should I budget for an old Tuscan farmhouse?

EmmePi's advice, reported by Your Overseas Home in 2026, is 10–15% for straightforward work, 15–20% for older rural property and about 20% for a full transformation. On a €250,000 build, 15% is €37,500 and 20% is €50,000. Stone walls, roofs and floors often hide problems that only appear once work starts.
