How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Italy in 2026? Prices by size and line by line

2026 prices for an Italian bathroom that needs a refresh or a full refit: ranges by size, a line-by-line worked example, how much to keep for surprises and which permits you need.

Updated 13 November 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

In 2026 a cosmetic bathroom refresh in Italy costs about €2,500–5,500. A full refit of a 4–6 m² bathroom costs €8,000–17,000, and can go past €20,000 with high-end materials or relocated drains. Add 10–15% on top for contingencies (sources: Edilizia.com and Il Giornale Edile).

The bathroom is the most expensive room per square metre: plumbing, waterproofing, tiling and sanitaryware are packed into a few m². This guide, updated in November 2026, covers what a bathroom renovation costs in Italy by type of work and by size, the individual line items and how to reach a realistic budget before you ask builders for quotes. Prepared by the staged.ai editorial team using figures from Edilizia.com and Il Giornale Edile, two Italian building trade sources. All prices are in euros and exclude VAT unless stated.

How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Italy in 2026?

The cost depends mostly on how much you redo. Telling a refresh apart from a full refit is the first step to a sound budget.

Type of work What it usually includes Typical cost 2026
Cosmetic refresh Sanitaryware, taps, vanity unit, mirror, paint; plumbing and tiles stay €2,500–5,500
Full bathroom (Il Giornale Edile) Strip-out, plumbing, tiling, sanitaryware, finishes €6,000–12,000
Full 4–6 m² bathroom (Edilizia.com) As above, plus waterproofing, a new shower and professional fees €8,000–17,000
Relocated drains or high-end finish New layout, premium materials and sanitaryware over €20,000

The two sources give different ranges for a full bathroom because they count different items: the higher estimate includes waterproofing, the professional and site coordination, which cheaper quotes often leave out. When you compare offers, always check what is inside.

The cost doesn't grow in line with the floor area: plumbing, sanitaryware and the shower cost roughly the same in a 4 or a 6 m² bathroom, while tiles and laying are what change. Location matters too: labour costs more in Milan than the national average and less in smaller towns. You'll find regional differences in the guide to renovation costs in Italy per m².

What makes a bathroom renovation cost more or less?

  • Moving the WC, shower or basin: relocating drains is the choice that raises the bill most, between building work and plumbing.
  • Bath or shower: replacing a bath with a walk-in, floor-level shower needs careful waterproofing and falls.
  • Tiling height: tiling up to the ceiling or only to 1.20 m makes a big difference to the tiles and the labour.
  • Condition of the services: in an older building it is wise to redo the plumbing even if it works today.
  • Wall-hung sanitaryware: easier to clean and more modern, but it needs in-wall frames and work on the walls.
  • Site access: upper floors without a lift and historic centres add time and waste disposal costs.

Do you need a permit to renovate a bathroom in Italy?

It depends on the work. Under Italy's building code DPR 380/2001, as summarised by Infobuild, replacing sanitaryware, floors and tiles without changing the layout is normally free building work (edilizia libera). If you move non-load-bearing partitions or make significant changes to the services, you generally need a CILA filed by a qualified professional, such as a geometra or architect. When the plumbing and electrical work is finished, ask for the certificate of conformity (dichiarazione di conformità). For tax deductions and reduced VAT, check the rules in force with a professional before you start.

How do you set a realistic budget before asking for quotes?

  1. Choose the type of work: cosmetic refresh or full refit. If the services are old, the choice is made for you.
  2. Measure the bathroom and the area to be tiled, and use the size table above as a first reference.
  3. Decide what stays where it is: keeping the WC, basin and shower in place is the simplest way to control costs.
  4. Visualise the result on a photo of your own bathroom, to choose tiles and sanitaryware before spending.
  5. Ask for at least three quotes on the same specification, with the items split out as in the worked example.
  6. Add 10–15% contingency, up to 25% in an older building.

Is it worth redoing the bathroom before selling or letting?

Not always. A dated bathroom weighs on how buyers see a property, but a full refit at €10,000–15,000 is rarely recovered in full in the sale price. A targeted cosmetic refresh is often the better call, or showing buyers what the bathroom could become with a visualisation and a cost estimate. Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to renovate a 5 m² bathroom in Italy?

For a full refit of a 5 m² bathroom (about 54 sq ft) with the same layout and a shower, Edilizia.com's 2026 estimate is €9,000–15,000, with a worked example of €13,500 before VAT and contingency. Il Giornale Edile puts a full bathroom at €6,000–12,000. The gap mostly comes down to plumbing, waterproofing and the quality of fittings and tiles.

How much does a bathroom refresh cost without stripping it out?

A cosmetic refresh, replacing sanitaryware, taps, the vanity unit and paint while keeping the plumbing and tiles, costs roughly €2,500–5,500 according to Edilizia.com. It is the most common choice before selling or letting, when the services are in good condition.

How much contingency should I set aside?

Allow 10–15% of the quote, rising to 15–25% in older buildings. Typical surprises in a bathroom are corroded pipes, crumbling screed and hidden damp, which only show up once the old tiles come off.

Do I need a CILA to renovate a bathroom in Italy?

Replacing sanitaryware and tiles without changing the room layout is normally free building work with no filing. If you move partition walls or make significant changes to the services, you generally need a CILA filed by a qualified professional, under Italy's building code DPR 380/2001. Always check with a geometra or architect and with the local town hall.
