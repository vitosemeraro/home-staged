Real estate listing photos: a practical guide to shooting homes that sell

Real estate listing photo tips: how to prep and shoot each room, how many photos to post, the order that gets clicks and how to enhance images with AI.

Updated 4 October 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

Good listing photos are bright, horizontal and straight. Declutter first, shoot in daylight with every light on, stand in a corner at roughly chest height and keep vertical lines vertical. Post at least 10 images, lead with the strongest rooms, and label any photo that has been virtually staged or edited beyond basic corrections.

Listing photos are the first viewing. Buyers and renters decide whether to get in touch by scrolling through images, usually on a phone and in seconds. You don't need professional kit to take good real estate listing photos, but you do need a method: prepare the home, shoot with the right light from the right spot, then publish enough images in the right order. This guide covers the practical rules, what portals expect, and how to use AI to improve photos without misleading anyone. Updated October 2026.

Why do listing photos matter so much?

Because they carry more weight than anything else in the listing. In the >NAR 2025 Profile of Home Staging, 73% of buyers' agents and 88% of sellers' agents said photos were much more or more important to their clients, ahead of physical staging, video and virtual tours. Portals agree: Italy's Immobiliare.it describes photos as the single element with the greatest weight on its listing quality score, which drives search ranking (>Immobiliare.it).

How many photos do you need, and at what quality?

At least 10, landscape, and comfortably above the portal's minimum resolution. Requirements vary by portal and MLS, so check yours. As a documented example, here is what Immobiliare.it asks of agencies:

Requirement Value Note
Minimum for a full photo score 10 images The first 10 carry the most weight
Maximum 200 images
Minimum resolution 1,024 px on the long side Below this, the photo score is halved
Maximum file size 32 MB per image JPEG, PNG, GIF
Orientation Landscape preferred
Overall listing quality Above 80% Listings above this threshold get 15–18% more contacts on average

Sources: >Immobiliare.it, Quality; >Immobiliare.it, Perfect photos. Both pages are in Italian.

How should you prepare a home before the shoot?

Preparation matters more than the camera. Before you shoot:

  • clear the clutter: empty kitchen worktops, no toiletries on show in the bathroom, no cables, shoes or laundry;
  • reduce personal items: family photos, fridge magnets, collections;
  • open curtains and blinds and switch on every light, including lamps and the cooker hood;
  • make the beds and straighten cushions and throws;
  • close toilet lids, hide bins, used towels and bath mats;
  • outside, move cars and wheelie bins away from the front.

NAR's data backs this up: decluttering is the most common recommendation agents give sellers (91%), followed by cleaning the whole home (88%). For a full room-by-room list, use our home staging checklist.

How do you photograph a room: light, height, framing?

The basics: plenty of natural light, a level camera, and a corner position.

  1. Light: shoot in daylight with the lights on to balance dark areas. Avoid direct sun through the window. Turn on HDR if your phone has it.
  2. Height: hold the camera at roughly chest height. Too high and furniture looks squashed; too low and the ceiling takes over.
  3. Straight verticals: door frames, walls and windows should be vertical. Turn on the grid and keep the phone parallel to the wall rather than tilting it up or down.
  4. Framing: stand in one corner and shoot towards the opposite one, taking in two or three walls. The room reads clearly and buyers see how it connects to the rest of the home.
  5. Moderate wide angle: it helps in small spaces, but an extreme wide angle distorts rooms and makes them look bigger than they are, which disappoints at the viewing.
  6. Orientation: landscape for portals. Save vertical crops for social media and stories.

Which photos should go first?

The first few images earn the click. A running order that works for most flats and houses:

  1. the living room or the brightest, most spacious area;
  2. the kitchen;
  3. the main bedroom;
  4. standout features: terrace, view, garden;
  5. other bedrooms, bathrooms and utility spaces;
  6. exterior, shared areas and the floor plan.

It is no coincidence that the rooms most often staged, according to NAR, are the living room (91%), primary bedroom (83%), dining room (69%) and kitchen (68%).

Are short-term rental photos different?

Partly. Airbnb and Booking guests aren't buying a space to transform; they are booking a ready-made stay. So on top of the rules above:

  • show beds and sofas ready to use, with good linen and soft furnishings;
  • include details that sell the stay: a work desk, coffee machine, view, a laid balcony table;
  • photograph every sleeping space, since guests want to know where everyone sleeps;
  • don't virtually add things guests won't find: in a short let, the promise in the photos is tested the evening they arrive.

Here AI is most useful for visualising a refresh before buying real furniture. More in virtual staging for Airbnb.

Is a professional photographer worth it?

It depends on the property. For a prime home, or if you manage many listings and want a consistent standard, a professional shoot is a sensible investment. For a mid-market flat, a recent smartphone and the method in this guide often get you most of the way. Frequently asked questions

Can I take real estate photos with my phone?

Yes. Recent smartphones produce images that easily meet portal requirements. Use the main camera or a moderate wide-angle lens, hold the phone level and horizontal, switch on the grid and HDR, and use a small tripod in dim rooms. Technique and preparation matter far more than the device for most mid-market listings.

What time of day is best for interior real estate photos?

For interiors, the middle of a bright day usually works well, with curtains open and every light switched on to fill dark corners. Avoid direct sun streaming through windows, which creates harsh contrast. For the exterior, check which way the front faces and shoot when it is lit, not in its own shadow.

How many photos should a property listing have?

Aim for at least 10, covering every room plus outside space and key features. Italian portal Immobiliare.it, for example, requires at least 10 images for a full photo score and weights the first 10 most heavily. Check your own portal or MLS for limits and minimum resolution, and put the strongest photos first.

Is it OK to edit or virtually stage listing photos?

Yes, as long as the result is not misleading. Exposure, colour and straightening are normal photo editing. Adding or removing furniture, or visualising renovations, should be clearly labelled with the original photo available. Since 2 August 2026, the EU AI Act requires disclosure of AI-generated or manipulated images that could pass as real.

Should listing photos be portrait or landscape?

Landscape for portals and MLS galleries, where it fills the frame and shows more of the room. Portrait 9:16 crops are useful for stories, reels and other vertical social formats. The practical approach is to shoot landscape and create vertical crops only for the channels that need them.
