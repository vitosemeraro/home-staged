Home staging statistics 2026: the verified numbers, with sources

Home staging statistics for 2026 with source, year and link for every figure: impact on offers and time on market, costs, listing photos and EU AI rules.

Updated 4 October 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

Home staging mainly helps buyers picture the home: in NAR's 2025 survey, 83% of buyers' agents said so, 29% of sellers' agents saw offers 1% to 10% higher and 30% saw a slight drop in time on market. Median spend on a US staging service is $1,500; AI virtual staging costs $0.25 to $3 per photo.

Does home staging work? The best available data says it mainly helps buyers picture themselves in the home and, in a meaningful share of cases, lifts offers and shortens time on market. This page brings together verifiable statistics on home staging, virtual staging, costs, listing photos and the rules on AI images, each with its source, year and link. We don't publish figures without a primary source you can check. Updated October 2026 by the editorial team at staged.ai (home-staged.ai).

Yes, according to most agents surveyed, though the effect varies. The most robust source is the 2025 Profile of Home Staging from the US National Association of REALTORS® (NAR).

What it means. The strongest, most widely shared effect is on perception: staging makes a home easier to imagine living in. Price and speed effects are reported by a sizeable minority of agents, not a majority, and they are agents' estimates rather than measured prices.

Methodology and limits

  • NAR 2025: survey sent in February 2025 to 49,806 agents, 1,266 usable responses, 2.5% response rate, margin of error ±2.75% at 95% confidence (>report PDF). It measures US agents' opinions, not transaction prices.
  • Costs: Cronoshare and Il Giornale Edile ranges are Italian market averages; AI Tools Bakery benchmarks are in US dollars and mostly US-based.
  • What we don't know: as of October 2026 we found no public, controlled European study on staging's effect on price or time on market. Very high "sells faster" percentages circulate online without a primary source you can check; we leave them out.

Frequently asked questions

Does home staging really increase the sale price?

The evidence points to a positive but not guaranteed effect. In the NAR 2025 Profile of Home Staging, 29% of sellers' agents reported offers 1% to 10% higher for staged homes compared with similar homes. These are US agents' perceptions rather than controlled experiments, so treat them as an indicator, not a promise for any single property.

How reliable is the NAR home staging survey?

It is the most widely cited source and publishes its methodology: a February 2025 survey sent to 49,806 agents, with 1,266 usable responses, a 2.5% response rate and a margin of error of plus or minus 2.75 points at 95% confidence. It measures agents' opinions, not actual sale prices, which is its main limitation.

Is there data comparing virtual staging with physical staging?

Only indirect data. In NAR's 2025 survey, 57% of buyers' agents rated traditional physical staging as much more or more important to clients, while 33% rated virtual staging as of less importance. Photos ranked highest at 73%. As of October 2026 we found no controlled study comparing the two methods on final sale price.

Are there European home staging statistics?

On costs, yes: Italian marketplace Cronoshare publishes 2026 price ranges, from €300 for basic staging to over €5,000 for a full project. On price and time-on-market effects, we found no European study with a public methodology comparable to NAR's as of October 2026, which is why the effectiveness data on this page is from the US and labelled as such.
