Virtual staging cost in 2026: AI vs designer vs physical staging

How much does virtual staging cost in 2026? Compare AI staging, designer-made virtual staging and physical staging, with a cost table and a worked example.

Updated 4 October 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

In 2026, AI virtual staging typically costs $0.25 to $3 per photo on a subscription, or about $1.10 to $2 per image pay-as-you-go. Virtual staging done by hand by a designer runs about $16 to $39 per image. Physical staging is far pricier: the NAR puts the median spend on a staging service at $1,500.

Virtual staging costs anything from a few cents to a few dozen dollars per photo, depending on whether software or a person does the work. In 2026, AI tools typically charge $0.25 to $3 per photo on a subscription, while a designer staging images by hand charges around $16 to $39 per image. Physical staging sits in a different league: hundreds or thousands per property, often plus monthly furniture rental. What hidden costs should you budget for?

  • Physical staging: delivery, installation and removal, furniture insurance, rental extensions if the sale drags on, touch-up painting and cleaning beforehand.
  • Designer virtual staging: extra revisions, rush delivery, additional formats for social media.
  • AI virtual staging: plans with few images included, paid re-generations, high-resolution downloads or watermark removal locked to higher tiers.
  • All options: your time to brief, choose and publish images, and to label them properly. Since 2 August 2026 the EU AI Act requires clear disclosure of AI-generated images that could pass as real; the UK and US rely on rules against misleading adverts. Everything is covered in our virtual staging disclosure rules guide.

Does staging pay for itself?

The best available evidence comes from the US. In the NAR 2025 survey, 29% of sellers' agents said staging brought offers 1% to 10% higher, and 30% saw a slight reduction in time on market. These are agents' views, not controlled studies, and there is no comparable European dataset yet. All the figures, with sources and years, are in our home staging statistics hub.

The maths is simple for virtual staging: at a few dollars per photo, one extra enquiry or one week less on the market is enough to recover the cost. For physical staging costing thousands, run the numbers per property, considering price point, buyer profile and local days on market.

How to choose by budget

  1. Near-zero budget, empty home: AI virtual staging of the key rooms, always labelled with the original photo alongside.
  2. Lived-in, cluttered home: free real-world decluttering first, then retouch or virtually empty the photos. See remove furniture from photos.
  3. Fixer-upper: show the potential with a renovation visualisation and a cost estimate, as in the renovation cost estimate from photos. Note that staged.ai cost estimates are calibrated on Italian prices.
  4. High-end property: consider physical staging in the two or three most-viewed rooms (living room, main bedroom, kitchen), because buyers will see it at viewings too.

Frequently asked questions

Is virtual staging worth the money?

For empty homes it usually is, because the cost is so low compared with the price of the property. In the NAR 2025 Profile of Home Staging, 83% of buyers' agents said staging made it easier for buyers to picture the home as theirs. Those are agent perceptions rather than controlled measurements, but at a few dollars a photo the bar for paying back is very low.

Who pays for virtual staging, the seller or the agent?

It varies by market and agreement. Physical staging is often paid by the seller or negotiated in the listing agreement. Because AI virtual staging costs so little, many agents simply include it in their service as a way to win listings. Whatever you decide, put it in writing so the seller knows which images will be virtual and how they will be labelled.

How much does it cost to virtually stage a whole house?

Multiply the number of rooms you want to show by the per-image price. Five rooms cost roughly $80 to $195 with a designer at $16 to $39 per image, or a few dollars with an AI subscription. Some tools charge per property analysis rather than per photo, so check what a credit actually covers before comparing plans.

Are there hidden costs with AI virtual staging?

Look at how many images a plan includes, whether re-generations use extra credits, and whether high-resolution downloads or watermark removal need a higher tier. Also budget for good source photos: AI results depend heavily on bright, straight, uncluttered shots. Finally, allow time to label staged images properly where disclosure rules apply.

Can I use Italian cost estimates if my property is outside Italy?

The visual staging works on any property, but cost estimates for renovation or clearance in staged.ai are calibrated on Italian market data. If your property is elsewhere, treat those figures as a rough reference only and get local contractor quotes. For homes in Italy, the city-based estimates are a useful starting point before a site visit.
