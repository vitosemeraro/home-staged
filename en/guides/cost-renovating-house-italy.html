How much does it cost to renovate a house in Italy? A 100 m² example for 2026

What a whole-house renovation in Italy adds up to in 2026: totals for a 100 m² home by level of work and by area, the big line items, how long the works and permits take, and how to set a realistic budget.

Updated 24 November 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

In 2026, renovating a 100 m² house or apartment in Italy (about 1,076 sq ft) costs roughly €70,000–100,000 for light works, €100,000–150,000 for a mid-level renovation and €150,000–200,000+ for a full one, using Il Giornale Edile's national averages. On the same source's city table, a full renovation of 100 m² runs €110,000–160,000 in Milan and €75,000–115,000 in the South. Expect 3–6 months on site (Edilizia.com), plus time for design and permits.

Per-square-metre prices are useful, but most people buying or owning a house in Italy want one number: what will the whole job cost? This guide works it out for a typical 100 m² home (about 1,076 sq ft), using 2026 figures from Il Giornale Edile and timings from Edilizia.com and Studio Madera, three Italian building trade sources. Prices are in euros. If you want the per-m² ranges for any size, see our guide to renovation costs in Italy per square metre.

On a larger home some fixed costs, such as site set-up, permits, bathrooms and the kitchen, are spread over more square metres, so the cost per m² tends to fall compared with a studio or a small flat.

How long does it take to renovate a 100 m² home in Italy?

According to Edilizia.com, a whole apartment usually takes three to six months on site:

Typical duration of renovation works in Italy
WorksTypical duration
Apartment of 70–100 m²3–6 months
House with structural work6–12 months or more
Full bathroom2–4 weeks
Kitchen and systems3–6 weeks

Source: Edilizia.com. These times don't always include design, permits, material orders and closing the paperwork.

Many individual jobs are short. For a 100 m² apartment, excluding masonry, Studio Madera estimates about 3 days to install the electrics, 2 days for radiator heating, 3 days for two coats of paint and around 10 days to redo a bathroom including demolition. Schedules stretch because of drying times, screeds, deliveries and coordinating several trades.

Which permits do you need, and how long do they take?

It depends on the works. Painting, new floors and replacing sanitaryware without changing the layout are usually free works with no filing. Moving internal partitions or significantly changing the systems generally needs at least a CILA, and heavier works a SCIA, under Italy's building code DPR 380/2001 (Infobuild).

  • CILA (notice of works, e.g. a new bathroom): 20–30 days of preparation (Studio Madera).
  • SCIA (heavier extraordinary maintenance): about 40 days (Studio Madera).

A licensed professional, usually a geometra, architect or engineer, prepares and files these with the town hall. Always check with them and with the local council which filing your project needs.

How do you get to a realistic budget before the quotes?

  1. Choose the level of work room by room. A 100 m² house is rarely "full" everywhere: you might redo the systems, bathrooms and kitchen and give the other rooms a light refresh.
  2. Use the range for the area, not the national average, if the house is in Milan or another large northern city.
  3. Keep a contingency. Edilizia.com suggests 10–15% of the quote, up to 25% in older buildings, where pipes, screeds and hidden damp often appear only after demolition.
  4. Put the paperwork in the calendar. Add the weeks for survey, design and filing to the time on site.
  5. Compare quotes on the same list of works, so the differences are about prices and materials, not about different jobs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the total cost of renovating a 100 m² home in Italy in 2026?

Using Il Giornale Edile's 2026 national averages, a 100 m² home costs about €70,000–100,000 to renovate lightly (€700–1,000 per m²), €100,000–150,000 for a mid-level job (€1,000–1,500 per m²) and €150,000–200,000 or more for a full renovation (€1,500–2,000+ per m²). Totals are floor area multiplied by the per-m² range.

Is it cheaper to renovate a house in the south of Italy than in Milan?

Usually, yes. Il Giornale Edile's 2026 city table puts a full renovation at €1,100–1,600 per m² in Milan, or €110,000–160,000 for 100 m², against €750–1,150 per m² in the South (around Naples or Bari), or €75,000–115,000, and €650–1,000 per m² in smaller towns.

How many months does a whole-house renovation in Italy take?

According to Edilizia.com, a 70–100 m² apartment usually needs 3–6 months on site, and 6–12 months or more if structural work is involved. Design, permits and material orders come on top. Studio Madera estimates 20–30 days to prepare a CILA and about 40 days for a SCIA.

How much should I keep aside for unexpected costs?

Edilizia.com suggests a contingency of 10–15% of the quote, rising to as much as 25% in older buildings, where pipes, screeds and hidden damp often only show up after demolition. On a €120,000 mid-level renovation, 10–15% means €12,000–18,000.

Do I need a permit to renovate a house in Italy?
