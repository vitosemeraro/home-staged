The home staging checklist: get every room ready for photos and viewings

A room-by-room home staging checklist to get your property ready for listing photos and viewings, backed by NAR 2025 data on what agents recommend most.

Updated 4 October 2026 · staged.ai editorial team · 6 min read

Quick answer

A good home staging checklist starts with three things: declutter, deep clean and tidy the entrance and exterior, the steps real estate agents recommend most (91%, 88% and 77%, NAR 2025). Then work room by room, prioritising the living room, main bedroom, dining room and kitchen, the rooms agents stage most often.

A home staging checklist turns "get the house ready" into a list you can actually work through: clear the clutter, clean properly, fix the small things and make every room easy to read in photos and at viewings. You don't need a big budget, just a method. This checklist, updated in October 2026, goes room by room and is built on what real estate agents (estate agents, if you're in the UK) recommend most often, according to the National Association of REALTORS® >2025 Profile of Home Staging.

Does home staging really make a difference?

Yes, if the goal is helping people picture the property as their own. In the NAR 2025 report, 83% of buyers' agents said staging does exactly that. Among sellers' agents, 29% saw offers increase by 1–10% and 30% saw a shorter time on the market (>PA Realtors summary). The data is from the US, but the principle travels: a tidy, bright home is easier to understand. More figures in our guide to home staging statistics.

The three steps agents recommend most are also the cheapest:

Recommendation Share of agents (NAR 2025) What it costs you
Declutter 91% Time
Deep clean 88% Time, or a cleaning company
Improve curb appeal 77% Little

What goes on the whole-house checklist?

Start with what applies everywhere, then move room by room.

  1. Depersonalise: family photos, collections, trophies, fridge magnets.
  2. Clear surfaces: worktops, tables, shelves, bedside tables. Two or three objects at most.
  3. Edit the furniture: if a room feels full, move a piece into storage.
  4. Deep clean: windows, grout, skirting boards, light fittings, switches.
  5. Fix small defects: loose handles, dripping taps, dead bulbs, misaligned cupboard doors.
  6. Even out the lighting: bulbs of the same colour temperature in every room.
  7. Tone down strong colours: a fresh coat of light, neutral paint is cheap and changes a lot.
  8. Deal with smells: air the rooms; avoid heavy air fresheners.

How do you stage the entrance and exterior?

Make it tidy and welcoming: it's the first impression at a viewing and often the lead photo of the listing. 77% of agents recommend working on it.

  • Clean front door, polished handle, house number easy to read.
  • A new, neutral doormat.
  • Balcony, terrace or front garden cleared of drying racks, boxes and tools.
  • One or two healthy plants.
  • Hallway free of shoes, coats and bags.

How do you stage the living room?

Create a clear, comfortable conversation area. It's the most commonly staged room, chosen by 91% of agents in the NAR report.

  • Arrange the sofa and chairs to face each other, not all pushed against the walls.
  • Hide cables and cut back on electronics.
  • A rug defines the space; a throw and two cushions add warmth.
  • Open curtains and blinds, and switch lights on even in daytime for photos.
  • Keep a clear path from the door to the window.

What matters most in the main bedroom?

Calm and order. It's the second most staged room (83%).

  • Light, freshly pressed bedding and an even number of pillows.
  • Almost empty bedside tables: a lamp and little else.
  • Tidy, half-empty wardrobes, because buyers open them.
  • No clothes on chairs.

How do you prepare the kitchen and dining room?

Clear surfaces and show the space working. The dining room (69%) and kitchen (68%) round out the most staged rooms.

  • Worktops cleared, with one decorative item at most.
  • Appliances clean, inside the oven and fridge too.
  • A simply laid table or a single centrepiece.
  • Chairs aligned and in proportion to the table.
  • If the kitchen is dated, try new handles and lighting before thinking about bigger works.

How do you make the bathroom spotless?

Focus on cleanliness and light, which is how bathrooms are judged in photos and in person.

  • Clean or redo grout and silicone.
  • White or neutral towels, neatly folded.
  • No personal products on show.
  • Mirrors and taps free of smears.
  • A new bath mat, and the toilet lid down in photos.

How do you handle the study, kids' rooms and storage?

Give every room one clear purpose. People struggle to value a room that's a junk room, office and guest room all at once.

  • Study or spare room: pick one use and furnish for it, say a desk and chair or a bed and bedside table.
  • Children's rooms: keep toys and posters to a minimum and the floor clear.
  • Storage, garage and loft: empty them at least partly; a packed storage space makes the home feel small.
  • Hallways and landings: remove stray furniture and keep pictures to one or two.
  • Utility room: lidded baskets, detergents put away, drying rack out of sight.

What should you check on photo or viewing day?

Do one last quick walk through every room before the photographer or the first visitor arrives:

  • all lights on and working, curtains open;
  • beds made, cushions plumped, clean towels out;
  • worktops and tables clear;
  • bins emptied, no bags on show;
  • pets, beds and bowls out of the shot;
  • cars moved off the drive if they'd appear in exterior photos;
  • windows opened for a few minutes to air the rooms, then closed if it's noisy outside.

What DIY staging mistakes should you avoid?

A few mistakes come up again and again and undo much of the work:

  • Hiding instead of clearing: stuffed wardrobes and cupboards get noticed at viewings.
  • Over-furnishing: the aim is to show the space, not the furniture.
  • Very personal colour schemes: bold walls and textiles distract buyers.
  • Ignoring the outside: the first photo and first impression start there.
  • Covering up defects: hiding damp or a crack is misleading and will come out eventually. Fix it or disclose it.

How should you photograph the house after staging?

Shoot in natural light, with the camera level at about chest height, and include two walls in each frame for depth. Good photos are what bring people to the viewing. Our guide to real estate listing photos covers it in detail.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do first when staging my home?

Declutter. Remove personal items, family photos, collections, surplus furniture and anything sitting on worktops and shelves. In the NAR 2025 Profile of Home Staging, decluttering was the most common recommendation sellers' agents made, cited by 91%. Deep cleaning (88%) and improving curb appeal (77%) came next. Only then is it worth thinking about furniture and accessories.

Which rooms matter most when staging a house?

The ones buyers focus on. According to the NAR 2025 report, the rooms most often staged are the living room (91%), the primary bedroom (83%), the dining room (69%) and the kitchen (68%). If time or budget is tight, concentrate on those, and make sure the bathroom is spotless even if you don't dress it.

Does home staging actually work?

The data is encouraging. In the NAR 2025 Profile of Home Staging, 83% of buyers' agents said staging helps buyers picture the property as their future home. Among sellers' agents, 29% saw offers rise by 1–10% and 30% saw a shorter time on the market. Results vary by market, but a tidy, bright home is easier to understand.

Can I stage a home for a rental the same way?

Mostly, yes. For long lets the same rules apply: clean, uncluttered, well-lit rooms help people picture themselves living there. For short-term rentals on Airbnb or Booking.com there's one more rule: guests must find exactly what the photos show. So stage the real property, and use AI images only to plan a restyle, always clearly labelled.

Should I combine DIY staging with virtual staging?

Often that's the most efficient route. Do the cheap things yourself, such as decluttering, cleaning and small repairs, then use virtual staging for empty rooms or rooms with dated furniture.
