Quick answer

A renovation cost estimate from photos is a first-pass budget built from pictures of the property. In staged.ai you upload up to 10 photos, choose the Renovation mode and get the room visualised after the works, a list of works with costs estimated for the Italian city, and a shareable quote. Always confirm with a site visit.

Before you book three builders for three site visits, you'd like at least a rough idea of the bill. Is the bathroom worth redoing? How much does new flooring in the living room add? A renovation cost estimate from photos gives you a first answer. staged.ai (home-staged.ai) is a web-based AI platform built for the Italian market: you upload photos, see the room after the works and get a list of works with costs estimated for the city. It won't replace a site visit, but you'll walk into that meeting knowing what you want.

One thing up front: the cost side is calibrated on Italian prices. If you're buying or renovating in Italy, that's exactly what you need. If your property is elsewhere, the visualisation still works, but treat the euro figures as a reference, not a local estimate.

What is a renovation cost estimate from photos?

It's a first-pass budget built from what the pictures show: surfaces, finishes, floors, tiling, bathroom fittings and fixed furniture. It gives you two things a simple per-square-metre calculator can't:

  • you see the result, the same room after the works, instead of imagining it;
  • costs come room by room, so you can decide where to spend and where not to.

How does staged.ai estimate the cost?

You upload up to 10 photos, choose a mode for each and set budget, style, city and a palette of real Sikkens paint colours.

  1. Upload the photos, for example those of a listing you're considering in Italy.
  2. Choose a mode: Renovation simulates new floors, walls and finishes with full works costs; Empty Room removes the furniture and estimates clearance, optionally including built-ins.
  3. Set budget and city: costs adjust to the local Italian market using sources such as OMI, Idealista, FIAIP and EAHSP.
  4. Read the room-by-room analysis: proposed works and the estimated cost of each item.
  5. Compare: create variants of a room, or project versions (v1, v2…) with different levels of work.
  6. Share or download the quote and a report with before/after and an estimated total.

Thanks to geometric lock, walls, doors, windows, kitchens and fixed bathroom fittings stay in place, with dedicated rules for kitchens and bathrooms, so you see new finishes on the real room rather than an invented one.

What does renovating in Italy cost in 2026?

According to >Il Giornale Edile, a light renovation in Italy costs about €700–1,000 per m² in 2026, a mid-level one €1,000–1,500 per m² and a full renovation €1,500–2,000+ per m². (One square metre is about 10.8 sq ft.) Typical line items:

Item Indicative cost, 2026
Painting €12–25/m²
Flooring and finishes €60–150/m²
Full bathroom €6,000–12,000
Electrical system €70–120/m²
Plumbing €80–140/m²

Location makes a big difference: for a full renovation the same source gives €1,100–1,600/m² in Milan and €750–1,150/m² in the South. Our guide to renovation costs in Italy has the full regional table and practical tips for foreign buyers.

How should you use the estimate with builders?

As a basis for comparison, not a price to impose. A few practical tips:

  • Bring the images: showing a builder the visualised room makes the finishes and level of work you have in mind clear straight away.
  • Use the list of works as a brief so you get quotes you can compare line by line.
  • Get several quotes: if one is far off the estimate, ask which items push it up or down.
  • Keep building works and furniture separate: works, services and furnishing involve different suppliers and timelines.

Used this way, a photo-based estimate becomes a conversation tool and helps you make sense of the quotes you receive.

Do the works need a permit in Italy?

It depends on the work, and that's something no photo can tell you. In short, under Italy's building code (DPR 380/2001), as summarised by >Infobuild:

  • free works (edilizia libera): painting, replacing floors, no permit needed;
  • CILA: modest internal works, such as moving non-load-bearing partitions;
  • SCIA: extraordinary maintenance touching structural elements;
  • building permit (permesso di costruire): major changes to volume, shape or use.

A qualified professional (geometra, architect or engineer) files CILA and SCIA. Budget for their fees too.

What are the limits of the estimate?

Photos don't show everything. Hidden services, damp, the state of floors and ceilings and planning compliance only come out on a site visit. staged.ai's estimates are Italian-calibrated and meant to guide your decision: get several quotes from real contractors before you sign. It doesn't produce floor plans, 3D renders or 360° tours. To see just the look of the works, start with virtual renovation; to clear the space first, try removing furniture from photos.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a renovation estimate from photos?

It's a ballpark, not a contractor's quote. Photos show floors, walls, finishes, bathroom fittings and furniture, but not hidden wiring and plumbing, damp, the state of the structure or planning constraints. Use it to understand the order of magnitude and compare options before calling builders. A binding price only comes after a site visit and a detailed bill of quantities.

Does the estimator work for properties outside Italy?

The visual part works anywhere, but the costs don't travel well. staged.ai's estimates are calibrated on the Italian market and the Italian city you choose, using sources such as OMI, Idealista, FIAIP and EAHSP, in euros. For a property in the UK, US, Australia or elsewhere, treat the figures as a rough reference only and get local quotes.

What does a renovation cost in Italy per square metre?

According to Il Giornale Edile, in 2026 a light renovation in Italy costs about €700–1,000 per square metre, a mid-level one €1,000–1,500 and a full renovation €1,500–2,000 or more. Prices vary by area: a full renovation runs about €1,100–1,600 per square metre in Milan and €750–1,150 in the South, for example in Naples or Bari.

Can I estimate clearance costs as well?

Yes. The Empty Room mode removes the furniture from the photo and produces a clearance quote, with an option to remove built-ins such as fitted kitchens and wardrobes too. That's handy when you're buying a furnished property or planning a new kitchen. You can then restage the empty room as a variant without using another credit.
